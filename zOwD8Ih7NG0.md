General information
-------------------

- Lorazepam is a commonly prescribed benzodiazepine for the
  management of insomnia and anxiety.

- Lorazepam works faster, with peak plasma levels in 1-2
  hours. Prazepam is absorbed more slowly, with effects
  developing over a longer period.


Lorazepam and the Risk of Serious Adverse Events
------------------------------------------------

<https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07179978>

Source: National Library for Biotechnology Information

Last update: 2025-09-24

> This is a population-based cohort study assessing whether
> initiating a new outpatient prescription of a higher dose
> of lorazepam (>1-4 mg/day) compared to a lower dose (0.5-1
> mg/day) in older adults with low kidney function (an eGFR
> <45 mL/min per 1.73 m2 but not receiving dialysis or
> having a history of kidney transplantation) is associated
> with an increased 30-day risk of a composite outcome,
> including all-cause hospitalization, all-cause emergency
> visit, or all-cause mortality.

> Despite warnings from Health Canada against prescribing
> benzodiazepines to older adults, approximately 1 in 9
> adults aged 65+ were prescribed a benzodiazepine in 2019
> in Ontario.

> **Prescribing references, product monographs, and other
> standard sources do not provide lorazepam daily dose
> recommendations in patients with low kidney function**. As
> a result, lorazepam is often initiated at the standard
> doses in patients with low kidney function.

> In patients with low kidney function, especially older
> adults, lorazepam can accumulate and increase the risk of
> developing serious adverse events.




No results are available yet; the analytical approach is
currently being designed: 
- The study will include approximately **3.2 million older
	adults**.  
- Participants will be stratified by baseline eG values:
	• eG ≥ 60 mL/min/1.73 m²  • 45 to < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²
	• < 45 mL/min/1.73 m²  
- New‑user and non‑user groups will be compared across **74
	clinically relevant outcomes**.  
- The non‑user cohort has a median size of **1,088,301
	individuals**.

Benzodiazepine use and mortality of incident dialysis patients in the United States
-----------------------------------------------------

Source: kidney international

<https://www.kidney-international.org/article/S0085-2538(15)52578-7/fulltext>

> Overall, 14% of incident dialysis patients used a
> benzodiazepine or zolpidem.

> In adjusted analyses, benzodiazepine or zolpidem use was
> associated with a 15% higher mortality rate.

> No association was found between benzodiazepine use and
> greater risk for hip fracture.

> We conclude that benzodiazepine or zolpidem use is common
> in incident dialysis patients and may be associated with
> greater mortality.


Usage patterns of benzodiazepines in a Dutch community: a 10-year follow-up
---------------------------------------------------------

Source: NIH <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9584341/>

> Use increased with age among both women and men. **Most of
> the users were 55 years or older.** One out of three
> patients was either an incidental user (1-30 days use in
> one calendar year), a regular (31-180 days), or a long
> term user (more than 180 days). **The use of long half-life
> hypnotics decreased, the use of the short half-life ones
> showed an increase.**

Lorazepam (FDA)
---------------

Source: FDA

<https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK532890/>

> Renal impairment: Manufacturers recommend taking
> precautions when using lorazepam in patients with renal
> disease, such as administering frequent doses over
> relatively short periods. According to a review, if
> benzodiazepines are required, lorazepam is a reasonable
> first-line choice for patients with end-stage renal
> disease.

> Lorazepam can cause CNS and respiratory depression in
> overdose, potentially leading to hypotension, ataxia,
> confusion, coma, extreme drowsiness, muscle weakness, and
> death.
